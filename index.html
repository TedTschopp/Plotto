The Plotto method

From a situation to a story.

01

Choose a pattern

A masterplot joins a protagonist, an action, and an outcome. The book offers 15 starting clauses, 62 actions, and 15 outcomes.

02

Follow the links

Every conflict points toward possible lead-ups and carry-ons. The outline follows those links, including suggested sequences, sections, and character changes.

03

Make it your own

Change the setting, motives, and people. These are prompts for your judgment: a carry-on can suggest an ending, or open another complication.

About the original text & this edition

William Wallace Cook published Plotto in 1928. The source includes dated language and prejudiced depictions. Both editions preserve that material; the reversed-role edition exchanges gendered terms and relationships.

The generator selects from the book’s existing suggestions. Character changes and transpositions are applied to the text; instructions to add a character remain visible as writing notes. Every lead-up and carry-on now names existing variants. Editorial repairs of broken printed links are labeled in the outline. The masterplot’s outcome is a direction for your writing, rather than a guarantee that every selected situation already supplies that ending.

Digital text cleaned by Gary Kacmarcik. The reference correction history distinguishes transcription fixes from editorial repairs, with original scan evidence. Read the project notes, full guide, or the original book at the Internet Archive.
